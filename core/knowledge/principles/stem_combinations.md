# Heavenly Stem Combinations (天干五合)

Separate from branch interactions, the ten Heavenly Stems combine in five
fixed pairs. Each pair is a Yang stem and a Yin stem, five positions apart in
the stem cycle (甲乙丙丁戊己庚辛壬癸), and each pair combines toward a
specific resulting element:

- 甲己合土 - Jia (Yang Wood) + Ji (Yin Earth) -> Earth
- 乙庚合金 - Yi (Yin Wood) + Geng (Yang Metal) -> Metal
- 丙辛合水 - Bing (Yang Fire) + Xin (Yin Metal) -> Water
- 丁壬合木 - Ding (Yin Fire) + Ren (Yang Water) -> Wood
- 戊癸合火 - Wu (Yang Earth) + Gui (Yin Water) -> Fire

## Combination vs. true transformation (合而不化 vs 合化)
Two stems sitting next to each other in a chart being a valid pair does not
automatically mean the pair *transforms* into the new element - traditional
theory treats this as a two-stage question:
1. **Combination (合)** always happens structurally when the pair is present
   and adjacent - it ties the two stems together, muting their individual
   Ten God expression to some degree even without transformation.
2. **Transformation (化)** into the new element additionally requires the
   surrounding chart to support it - commonly cited conditions are that the
   birth month (season) matches or generates the resulting element, and that
   the resulting element isn't strongly opposed elsewhere in the chart. When
   these conditions aren't met, the standard read is "combined but not
   transformed" (合而不化) - the tie/muting effect still applies, but the
   stems don't functionally become the new element.

## Why the Day Master's own combination matters most
When the Day Master itself combines with an adjacent stem, it is
traditionally read as significant regardless of whether true transformation
occurs - the "self" is being pulled toward, tied to, or distracted by
whatever that other stem represents (a Ten God, and by extension a life
area: a Wealth-star combination pulling toward money/partner, an
Officer-star combination pulling toward duty/authority, etc.). Some
traditions read an unwanted Day Master combination as the person being
"controlled" or "not fully themselves" during that influence, which is
especially relevant if the combining stem arrives via a Da Yun or yearly
pillar rather than being natal.

## Practical reading note
As with branch combinations, only flag a stem combination as meaningful when
it's actually adjacent in the chart (stems in non-adjacent pillars combining
is a much weaker, often-ignored effect in practice) or when a Da Yun/yearly
stem newly combines with a natal stem - especially the Day Master.
