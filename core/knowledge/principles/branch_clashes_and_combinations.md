# Branch Clashes and Combinations (地支刑沖合會)

Earthly Branches don't just sit independently in a chart - specific pairs
interact with each other across pillars, and these interactions can activate,
suppress, or transform the elements/Ten Gods involved. This is one of the
more advanced layers of a reading and is easy to overdo (not every possible
pair in a chart is meaningfully "active").

## Six Clashes (六沖) - opposing branches, 6 apart on the 12-branch wheel
Zi-Wu, Chou-Wei, Yin-Shen, Mao-You, Chen-Xu, Si-Hai.
A clash is generally disruptive/unsettling for whatever the two branches
represent (the pillars they sit in, and any Ten Gods/hidden stems tied to
them). Clashes involving the Day branch specifically often relate to
instability in one's own foundation, health, or spouse relationship
(the Day branch is traditionally the "spouse palace").

## Six Combinations (六合) - paired branches that combine, often into a
different resulting element
Zi-Chou (-> Earth), Yin-Hai (-> Wood), Mao-Xu (-> Fire), Chen-You (-> Metal),
Si-Shen (-> Water), Wu-Wei (no transformation, near-neutral).
A combination is generally harmonizing - it can also "tie up" or distract the
two branches involved from acting independently, which is sometimes a
downside (e.g. a Day branch combination pulling attention away from career
matters toward relationship matters, or vice versa).

## Three Harmonies (三合) - three branches forming a strong single element
Shen-Zi-Chen (-> Water), Hai-Mao-Wei (-> Wood), Yin-Wu-Xu (-> Fire),
Si-You-Chou (-> Metal).
When most or all three branches of a set appear across a chart (natal +
Da Yun/year), it strongly reinforces that element - a significant amplifying
signal, more so than a simple two-branch combination.

## Three Punishments (三刑)
Punishments are a subtler, more ambiguous interaction than a clash -
traditionally read as friction, ingratitude, legal/conflict trouble, or
self-sabotage between the branches involved, but generally weaker and more
context-dependent than a direct clash. Three groupings:
- **Punishment of Bullying/Power (恃勢之刑)**: Yin-Si-Shen (寅-巳-申), a
  three-way mutual punishment.
- **Punishment of Ingratitude (無恩之刑)**: Chou-Xu-Wei (丑-戌-未), also a
  three-way mutual punishment.
- **Punishment of Rudeness (無禮之刑)**: Zi-Mao (子-卯), a mutual pair
  rather than a triangle.
- **Self-Punishment (自刑)**: a branch punishing itself when it repeats -
  Chen-Chen (辰辰), Wu-Wu (午午), You-You (酉酉), Hai-Hai (亥亥).

## Six Breaks (六破)
A milder, more debated interaction across different traditional schools -
some sources treat it as near-negligible, others use it as a secondary
timing signal for things not fully materializing or plans "breaking down."
Given the lower consensus on this one, treat it as the weakest-confidence
interaction type here: Zi-You (子酉), Wu-Mao (午卯), Si-Shen (巳申),
Hai-Yin (亥寅), Chen-Chou (辰丑), Xu-Wei (戌未).

## Practical reading note
Only flag a clash/combination/punishment/break as meaningful when it
directly involves pillars relevant to the question (e.g. Day branch for
relationship questions, Month branch for career/timing questions), or when
a Da Yun/yearly branch newly activates one against the natal chart. Listing
every theoretically possible interaction in a chart produces noise, not
insight - and weight these roughly in order of strength: clash and
three-harmony first, then combination and punishment, then break last.
